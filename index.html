ABOUT

About Me

I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Geosciences at The Pennsylvania State University, working with Dr. Miquela Ingalls. My dissertation develops siderite oxygen and clumped isotopes as an archive of terrestrial paleoclimate and diagenesis. More broadly, I use low-temperature stable isotope geochemistry — including carbonate clumped isotopes — to read the thermal, hydrological, and environmental histories preserved in sedimentary rocks, and to understand how those signals are altered during burial and diagenesis. Before Penn State, I earned my B.S. in Geology (Rank 1/86) from the China University of Geosciences (Beijing).

RESEARCH

Research Interests

01

Siderite Clumped Isotopes

Developing iron carbonate (siderite) oxygen and clumped isotope (Δ47) thermometry as a terrestrial paleoclimate proxy, and quantifying how burial and solid-state reordering alter the recorded signal.

02

Diagenesis & Water–Rock Interaction

Using petrographic and geochemical core–outcrop comparisons to track how carbon, oxygen, clumped, and Mg isotopes respond to fluid-mediated burial and late-stage meteoric diagenesis.

03

Paleoclimate & Biogeochemistry

Reconstructing continental temperatures, marine sulfate, and carbon cycling — from the Cryogenian Snowball Earth to the Paleocene — using stable isotopes and box modeling.
